Glossary of difficult terms (171 entries)

Dotted-underlined words in the text also show this definition on hover or tap.

  • ACL — Anterior Cruciate Ligament - a key ligament controlling forward glide of the tibia.
  • action potential — The brief electrical impulse a nerve or muscle cell fires when stimulated.
  • adhesive capsulitis — Frozen shoulder - a painful, progressive stiffening of the shoulder joint capsule.
  • aerobic — Exercise using oxygen as the main energy source (lower intensity, longer duration).
  • amputation — Surgical removal of a limb or part of a limb.
  • anaerobic — Exercise using energy systems that do not need oxygen (higher intensity, shorter duration).
  • ankylosing spondylitis — An inflammatory arthritis of the spine that can fuse vertebrae and flatten the back's curves.
  • anthropometry — The measurement of the size, weight and proportions of the human body.
  • anti-gravity muscles — Tonic postural muscles that keep the body upright against gravity.
  • arthroplasty — Surgical reconstruction or replacement of a joint.
  • ASES — American Shoulder and Elbow Surgeons score - a shoulder function questionnaire.
  • assistive devices — Tools or equipment that help a person perform tasks more easily or safely.
  • ATP-PC — The immediate energy system using stored ATP and phosphocreatine for short bursts.
  • autonomous practice — Physiotherapy practice with independent clinical decision-making and accountability.
  • back school — A structured education programme teaching people to manage back pain themselves.
  • below-knee amputation — Removal of the leg below the knee, preserving the knee joint.
  • BIA — Bioelectrical Impedance Analysis - estimating body composition from how current passes through tissue.
  • biofeedback — Using a device to show a patient their own body signals so they can control them.
  • Bloom's Taxonomy — A hierarchy of learning objectives from remembering up to creating.
  • BMI — Body Mass Index - weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared.
  • body composition — The proportions of fat and lean (muscle, bone, water) tissue in the body.
  • Butler — David Butler - a key figure behind neurodynamic (neural mobilisation) testing and treatment.
  • carpal tunnel syndrome — Compression of the median nerve at the wrist, causing numbness and weakness in the hand.
  • CBT — Cognitive Behavioural Therapy - a talking therapy that changes unhelpful thoughts and behaviours.
  • central tendency — The typical or middle value of a set of data (mean, median or mode).
  • Chi-Square — A statistical test comparing observed and expected frequencies in categories.
  • chronaxie — The time for which a current of twice the rheobase must flow to stimulate a nerve or muscle.
  • CMRR — Common Mode Rejection Ratio - how well an amplifier cancels electrical noise common to both inputs.
  • cohort — A study following a defined group of people over time to see who develops an outcome.
  • Colles fracture — A fracture of the lower end of the radius at the wrist, often after a fall on the hand.
  • competency-based education — Training that focuses on demonstrating defined practical competencies rather than time spent.
  • CRPS — Complex Regional Pain Syndrome - severe, persistent pain and changes in a limb after injury.
  • DASH — Disabilities of the Arm, Shoulder and Hand - an upper-limb function questionnaire.
  • deltoid — The large shoulder muscle that lifts the arm.
  • determinants of gait — The six normal features that keep walking energy-efficient and smooth.
  • DEXA — Dual-Energy X-ray Absorptiometry - a scan measuring bone density and body fat.
  • differential amplifier — An amplifier that boosts the difference between two inputs while cancelling shared noise.
  • direct access — Patients seeing a physiotherapist without first being referred by a doctor.
  • dry needling — Thin needles inserted into trigger points to relieve muscle pain.
  • dysfunction — Spinal pain from shortened or scarred tissue, felt mainly at end-range.
  • electrotherapy — Treatment using electrical energy, such as TENS, IFT or electrical stimulation.
  • EMG — Electromyography - recording the electrical activity of muscles.
  • energetics — The study of energy use, efficiency and metabolic cost in movement.
  • energy conservation — Techniques that reduce the effort and energy needed to complete activities.
  • EPOC — Excess Post-exercise Oxygen Consumption - raised oxygen use after exercise as the body recovers.
  • ergonomics — Designing the workplace and tasks to fit the worker and reduce strain.
  • ESWT — Extracorporeal Shockwave Therapy - high-energy acoustic pulses applied to a tendon to stimulate healing.
  • ethnography — A qualitative approach studying a group's culture through close observation.
  • FCE — Functional Capacity Evaluation - a standardised assessment of ability to perform work tasks.
  • fibromyalgia — A chronic condition of widespread pain, fatigue and tenderness.
  • flexor tendon repair — Surgery to rejoin a cut tendon that bends the fingers or thumb.
  • foot-drop — Weakness of ankle dorsiflexion, causing the foot to drag or slap when walking.
  • force closure — Stability of the sacroiliac joint from muscles and ligaments compressing it.
  • forward head posture — Head positioned ahead of the shoulders, increasing load on the neck.
  • FRAX — A tool estimating the 10-year risk of a fragility fracture.
  • gait — The manner of walking.
  • Garden classification — A grading of hip (femoral neck) fracture displacement, from I to IV.
  • glycolysis — Breakdown of glucose to release energy without oxygen.
  • grounded theory — A qualitative method that builds a theory from patterns found in the data.
  • Henneman — The size principle: motor units are recruited from smallest to largest as force rises.
  • HLA-B27 — A genetic marker associated with ankylosing spondylitis.
  • hydrotherapy — Exercise or treatment carried out in warm water.
  • hypothesis — A testable prediction about the relationship between variables.
  • ICF — International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health - the WHO framework for describing health.
  • ICMR — Indian Council of Medical Research - issues national ethics guidelines for health research in India.
  • IFT — Interferential Therapy - two medium-frequency currents crossing to give a pain-relieving effect.
  • impingement — Pinching of tissue (often the rotator cuff) beneath the shoulder's bony roof.
  • interval — A measurement scale with equal intervals but no true zero (e.g. temperature in Celsius).
  • IVDP — Intervertebral Disc Prolapse - a disc bulging and pressing on nerves.
  • joint play — The small accessory gliding movement at a joint that is necessary for normal motion.
  • kinematics — The description of motion (positions, angles, velocities) without reference to forces.
  • kinetics — The forces that cause or result from motion.
  • kyphosis — An excessive outward (forward) curve of the thoracic spine.
  • lateral epicondylalgia — Tennis elbow - pain at the outer elbow from a tendon problem at the ECRB origin.
  • LLLT — Low-Level Laser Therapy - red or near-infrared light that stimulates cell repair (photobiomodulation).
  • lower cross syndrome — A postural pattern of tight hip flexors and lower back with weak abdominals and gluteals.
  • Maitland grades — A I-V scale of passive joint mobilisation, from small pain-relieving to manipulative techniques.
  • McKenzie — The Mechanical Diagnosis and Therapy approach classifying spinal pain by response to repeated movement.
  • MDT — Mechanical Diagnosis and Therapy - the McKenzie method of assessing and treating spinal pain.
  • median nerve — A nerve in the arm supplying the thumb-side hand muscles and sensation.
  • meniscus — The C-shaped cartilage pad in the knee that cushions and stabilises the joint.
  • motor unit — A single motor nerve and all the muscle fibres it supplies.
  • Mulligan — A manual therapy approach applying a sustained glide while the patient moves (Mobilisation with Movement).
  • myofascial release — Manual pressure and stretch applied to muscle and fascia to reduce tightness (MFR).
  • NCS — Nerve Conduction Study - measuring how fast electrical signals travel along a nerve.
  • neurodynamics — The mechanical and physiological behaviour of nerves; neural mobilisation treats nerve sensitivity.
  • neuromatrix — The theory that pain is produced by a widely distributed brain network, not just by tissue input.
  • Newton's Second Law — Force equals mass times acceleration (F = ma).
  • NMES — Neuromuscular Electrical Stimulation - using electrical current to make muscles contract.
  • nociceptive — Relating to the detection of painful stimuli by the nervous system.
  • nociplastic — Pain arising from altered pain processing without clear tissue or nerve damage.
  • nominal — A measurement scale that only labels categories, with no order (e.g. blood group).
  • non-parametric — Statistical tests that make no assumption about the data's distribution.
  • normal distribution — The symmetrical bell-shaped spread of data around a mean.
  • observational — A study that observes people without the researcher assigning any treatment.
  • ODI — Oswestry Disability Index - a questionnaire measuring disability from low back pain.
  • ordinal — A measurement scale that ranks order but not equal intervals (e.g. pain mild/moderate/severe).
  • orthotics — External supports such as insoles or braces used to correct alignment and offload tissue.
  • osteoporosis — Loss of bone density that makes bones fragile and prone to fracture.
  • osteosarcoma — A type of bone cancer that most often affects young people.
  • Ottawa rules — A decision guide indicating when an ankle or foot X-ray is needed after injury.
  • outcome measures — Standardised tools used to measure change in a patient's symptoms or function.
  • oxidative phosphorylation — Energy production in mitochondria using oxygen.
  • p-value — The probability of getting the observed result if the null hypothesis were true.
  • pacing — Spreading activity evenly with planned rests to avoid pain flares.
  • parametric — Statistical tests that assume the data follow a particular distribution, usually normal.
  • patellectomy — Surgical removal of the kneecap (patella).
  • PCL — Posterior Cruciate Ligament - a knee ligament limiting backward glide of the tibia.
  • PEMF — Pulsed Electromagnetic Field therapy - pulsed magnetic fields used to promote tissue and bone healing.
  • phenomenology — A qualitative approach studying people's lived experience of a phenomenon.
  • photobiomodulation — The use of light to alter cell activity and promote healing.
  • physical activity — Any bodily movement that uses energy, such as walking, cycling or sport.
  • pilot study — A small trial run of a study to test and refine the methods before the main one.
  • PNE — Pain Neuroscience Education - teaching patients how pain works in the nervous system.
  • posture — The alignment and positioning of the body's segments relative to each other and gravity.
  • primary care — The first point of contact for health advice and treatment in the community.
  • psychometric — Relating to the measurement properties (validity, reliability) of a scale.
  • Q-angle — The angle between the thigh and the patellar tendon, affecting knee-cap tracking.
  • Q10 — The temperature coefficient - the factor by which a metabolic rate rises per 10°C increase.
  • qualitative research — Research exploring meaning, experience and themes rather than numbers.
  • quasi-experimental — A study design with an intervention but without full random assignment.
  • radicular — Pain arising from compression or irritation of a nerve root.
  • ratio — A measurement scale with equal intervals and a true zero (e.g. weight, range of motion).
  • RCT — Randomised Controlled Trial - participants are randomly assigned to treatment or control.
  • REBA — Rapid Entire Body Assessment - a tool scoring whole-body postural risk at work.
  • red flags — Warning signs suggesting serious underlying disease rather than a simple mechanical problem.
  • reliability — The consistency of a measurement across repeated occasions or raters.
  • resting potential — The electrical charge difference across a nerve or muscle membrane at rest.
  • rheobase — The smallest current, applied for a long time, that just produces a muscle/nerve response.
  • Roland-Morris — A questionnaire measuring disability from low back pain.
  • rotator cuff — The group of four shoulder muscles and tendons that stabilise the shoulder joint.
  • RULA — Rapid Upper Limb Assessment - a tool scoring posture risk for the upper limbs and neck.
  • sacroiliac joint — The joint where the spine meets the pelvis.
  • sample size — The number of participants included in a study.
  • sampling — Selecting a subset of people from a population to study.
  • scapulohumeral rhythm — The coordinated ratio of shoulder-blade and shoulder-joint movement during arm raise.
  • Scheuermann's — A spinal disorder of adolescence causing wedging of vertebrae and thoracic kyphosis.
  • scoliosis — A sideways curvature of the spine.
  • screening — A quick examination to decide whether a region needs deeper assessment or onward referral.
  • screw-home mechanism — The small automatic rotation of the knee that locks it in full extension.
  • sensory integration — The brain combining input from vision, balance and body sense to control posture.
  • single-subject — A design that studies one participant in depth over time, often with repeated phases.
  • skinfold — Callipers measuring fat thickness at set body sites to estimate body fat.
  • SPADI — Shoulder Pain and Disability Index - a questionnaire scoring shoulder pain and function.
  • spatiotemporal — Relating to both distance and timing parameters of gait, such as step length and cadence.
  • spinal cord injury — Damage to the spinal cord that impairs movement, sensation or body functions (SCI).
  • Spurling — A neck test where the head is tilted and pressed down to provoke nerve-root pain.
  • supracondylar — Relating to the area just above the knuckles of a bone, such as above the elbow.
  • SWD — Shortwave Diathermy - deep heating of tissue using high-frequency electromagnetic energy.
  • Syme's — An ankle-level amputation that removes the foot but keeps a weight-bearing heel pad.
  • T-score — How far bone density lies from a young healthy adult average, in standard deviations.
  • taping — Applying therapeutic tape to support tissue, limit movement or give feedback.
  • tendoachilles — The Achilles tendon, joining the calf muscles to the heel bone.
  • tendon transfer — Surgery moving a working tendon to take over the action of a paralysed muscle.
  • tennis elbow — Lateral elbow pain from a tendon problem at the forearm extensor origin.
  • TENS — Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation - a mild current through the skin to relieve pain.
  • THR — Total Hip Replacement - surgical replacement of the hip joint.
  • tissue extensibility — The ability of tissue to be lengthened or stretched.
  • TKR — Total Knee Replacement - surgical replacement of the knee joint.
  • TLSO — Thoraco-Lumbo-Sacral Orthosis - a brace supporting the mid and lower spine.
  • torque — A turning force around an axis - the product of force and its moment arm.
  • trapezitis — Painful tightness and inflammation of the upper trapezius muscle at the neck and shoulder.
  • Trendelenburg — A drop of the opposite hip when standing on one leg, indicating hip abductor weakness.
  • Tyler's principles — A curriculum model built around objectives, learning experiences, organisation and evaluation.
  • ULNT — Upper Limb Neurodynamic Test - moving the arm to tension a nerve and detect sensitivity.
  • upper cross syndrome — A postural pattern of tight upper neck and chest muscles with weak deep neck flexors.
  • validity — The extent to which a tool measures what it is intended to measure.
  • WCPT — World Physiotherapy (formerly World Confederation for Physical Therapy) - the global physiotherapy body.
  • work conditioning — A work-focused programme building general strength, endurance and mobility for return to work.
  • work hardening — An intensive, job-specific programme that simulates real work tasks.
  • Z-score — How far bone density lies from the average of a person's own age group, in standard deviations.
